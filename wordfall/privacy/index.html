Wordfall

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 19, 2026

Wordfall is a word game for iPhone and iPad made by Riley Broderick, an independent developer in the United States ("I", "me"). This policy explains what information the game collects, why, who receives it, how long it's kept, and the choices you have. It applies to the Wordfall app and this website.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. I never ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or location.
  • The game can send anonymous play data (things like "level 12 won, 3 stars") filed under a random ID made on your device. You can turn it off any time in Settings › More › Share anonymous play data. In Europe (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland) it's off unless you turn it on.
  • Ads come from Google AdMob. They're limited to general-audience content and aren't personalized unless you allow tracking when iOS asks.
  • Purchases are handled by Apple. I never see your payment details or Apple Account.
  • Your progress is kept on your device and in your own iCloud account. Leaderboards use Game Center. I can't see either.
  • I don't sell your information.

1. What the game collects

Anonymous play data (Wordfall's own analytics)

To see which levels are too hard, too easy or confusing, the game records short game events, for example a level started or finished, a mode played, a setting changed, coins earned or spent, an offer shown or taken, an ad shown, or a purchase made. Each event contains:

  • a random play-data ID made the first time play data is turned on (it isn't tied to your device's hardware, your Apple Account or your advertising ID);
  • the event's name and time, the app version and the platform (for example "ios");
  • game details such as the level, score, stars, word counts, how long a level took, the coins involved, and the product and price of a purchase. A few events include a word, for example the first word you ever play or a word you look up in the dictionary.

Events wait on your device and are sent in small batches over an encrypted connection to a database I run on Supabase. The game's key for that database can only add new events. It can't read, change or delete anything. Like any web server, Supabase sees your IP address when the events arrive. It isn't stored with your play data, and Supabase's request logs, which may include it, are deleted automatically within 7 days.

The same switch also controls Google Analytics for Firebase, which receives the main game events (level start and end, offers, ads, purchases, coins earned and spent, the tracking-permission answer, and a few first-session milestones) so I can see trends in Google's reports. Firebase adds some information of its own: a random app-instance ID, your device model, iOS version, app version and language, your general location (country or region, estimated from your IP address), when you first opened the app, session counts and engagement time, and App Store purchases (product, price and currency). Wordfall doesn't give Firebase your advertising ID or your device's vendor ID, and Firebase's advertising features are switched off, so this data isn't used for ads.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Wordfall shows ads from Google AdMob: rewarded videos only when you choose to watch one, and, from level 21, an occasional full-screen ad between levels (never with Remove Ads). To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google's software collects information such as your IP address, device and app identifiers, the ads you see and tap, approximate location estimated from your IP address, and app performance and crash information.

  • Not personalized unless you allow tracking. After level 10, the game explains tracking and iOS asks whether Wordfall may track you. Until you choose "Allow", ad requests ask for non-personalized ads and Google can't use Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA). If you allow it, Google may use the IDFA and your activity to personalize ads.
  • Consent where the law asks for it. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form appears before any ad loads. Where the law gives you privacy choices, Settings › More › Privacy choices lets you change them.
  • General audiences only. Ads are limited to content rated for general audiences.

Google uses this information under its own privacy policy. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Purchases (Apple and RevenueCat)

Purchases (coin packs, the starter bundle, Remove Ads and the season pass) are made through Apple's App Store. Apple handles payment, and I never see your card details, name or Apple Account. To check that a purchase is real, deliver it, and let you restore it, the game uses RevenueCat, which receives your App Store receipt and transaction details (what was bought, when, the price and Apple's transaction IDs) under a random, anonymous customer ID, along with your device model, app version, when the app was last opened, and your IP address (from which RevenueCat estimates a general location). This isn't connected to your name, email or Apple Account.

Apple services

  • iCloud. Your progress, stars, coins, items, streak and a record of purchases already delivered are copied to iCloud key-value storage, so they survive a reinstall and appear on your other devices. This is stored in your iCloud account by Apple, and I can't access it.
  • Game Center. If you're signed in to Game Center, your scores go to Wordfall's leaderboards (today's puzzle, the weekend event, Sprint and total stars) under your Game Center nickname. If you allow it when iOS asks, the game shows how your Game Center friends did. Their names and scores are only shown on your device. They're never sent to me, and the game never posts or notifies anyone about them. Apple handles this under its Game Center privacy notice.
  • Notifications. If you turn on the daily reminder, it's scheduled on your device. No information is sent to a server for it.
  • Sharing. When you share a result, iOS's share sheet sends the picture and text only where you choose.

Kept only on your device

Your settings, progress and recently looked-up words are saved in the app on your device (and the parts listed above in iCloud). Play events waiting to be sent are stored on your device until they're sent, up to 1,000 at most.

If you contact me

If you email me, I receive your email address and whatever you write, and use them only to reply and to handle your request.

2. What it doesn't collect

Wordfall doesn't collect your name, email address (unless you email me), phone number, contacts, precise location, photos, camera or microphone, health or fitness information, or payment details. There's no account to create, no chat and no way for other players to message you.

3. Why it's used

  • To run the game and deliver what you buy: purchases, restoring them, syncing progress, leaderboards and reminders.
  • To improve the game: play data shows where levels are unfair, where players get stuck or quit, and whether changes help.
  • To pay for the game with ads, and to measure ads and prevent ad fraud (Google).
  • To keep things secure and working, and to meet legal obligations.

If you're in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, the legal bases are: your consent for play data and Google Analytics (you turn them on), for tracking (iOS's prompt), and for ads as you choose in Google's consent form; performance of a contract for delivering and restoring purchases; and legitimate interests for keeping the game secure and working. You can withdraw consent at any time (see Your choices). That doesn't affect anything done before.

5. Your choices

  • Play data: Settings › More › Share anonymous play data turns off both Wordfall's analytics and Google Analytics. Turning it off deletes events still waiting on your device and stops new ones.
  • Tracking: answer "Ask App Not to Track" when iOS asks, or change it any time in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking.
  • Ad privacy choices: where shown, Settings › More › Privacy choices reopens Google's consent options.
  • No ads between levels: Remove Ads turns off the ads between levels.
  • Game Center and friends: manage them in iOS Settings › Game Center.
  • Reminders: turn them off in the game's settings or in iOS Settings › Notifications.
  • Deleting the app removes everything stored in the app on that device. Your iCloud copy stays in your iCloud account.

Wordfall doesn't respond to web browsers' "Do Not Track" signals, since it isn't a website. The controls above are how you choose.

6. How long it's kept

  • Play data in my database: deleted automatically 24 months after it's recorded, or sooner if you ask.
  • Google Analytics: event-level data is kept for 2 months, after which only aggregated reports that don't identify anyone remain.
  • Server request logs (Supabase): 7 days at most.
  • Purchase records (RevenueCat): as long as Wordfall uses RevenueCat, so purchases can be checked and restored, or until you ask me to delete them.
  • Ads data: kept by Google under its own retention policies (see how Google retains data).
  • Emails: as long as needed to answer you and keep a record of your request, and no longer than 2 years.
  • On your device and in iCloud: until you delete the app (device) or your iCloud data (iCloud).

7. Your rights and how to use them

Wherever you live, you can ask me to:

  • tell you what play data I hold about you and give you a copy;
  • delete it;
  • stop using it, or withdraw your consent.

Depending on where you live, you may have more rights. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, these include correction, restriction, objection and portability, and the right to complain to your data protection authority. In California and other US states with privacy laws, these include the right to know, delete, correct, and opt out of targeted advertising, "sale" or "sharing", and the right not to be treated differently for using them.

How to make a request: because there are no accounts, play data is filed only under the random play-data ID. In the game, go to Settings › More › Copy play-data ID, then email it to riley.brodericka@gmail.com and say what you'd like. The ID is how I find your data, so I won't ask for anything else to identify you. If play data was never turned on, there's no ID and nothing stored by me. I'll reply within 30 days. If I can't do what you ask, I'll explain why, and you can appeal by replying with "Appeal". I'll answer an appeal within 45 days. An authorized agent may make a request for you with your written permission.

Google Analytics, AdMob and RevenueCat data is held under random identifiers that I can't connect to you or to your play-data ID. Turning off play data stops Google Analytics, iOS's tracking setting and Privacy choices control ads, and I can ask RevenueCat to delete purchase records for a purchase you identify (for example from your App Store receipt). You can also contact Google or RevenueCat directly.

8. Children

Wordfall is a general-audience game and isn't directed at children under 13. I don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 (or under the age of digital consent where you live). Ads are limited to general-audience content. If you believe a child has used Wordfall and you'd like their play data deleted, email me with their play-data ID as described above and I'll delete it.

9. Where data is processed

I'm based in the United States, and the services above process data in the United States and other countries. Where information from the EEA, the UK or Switzerland is transferred, those services rely on recognized safeguards, such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (and its UK and Swiss extensions) where they're certified, or the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

10. Security

Information is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The game's key for my database can only add new events and can't read anything back, and only I can view the data. No system is perfectly secure, but I take reasonable steps to protect what's collected and keep it to the minimum the game needs.

If this policy changes, I'll post the new version here and update the date at the top. If a change materially affects how your information is used, I'll also say so in the app or in the App Store release notes before it applies, and ask for your consent where the law requires it.
